## C

The stored cyclic order is counter-clockwise: the next slot is counter-clockwise, as on the convention page. Inferred from that convention together with the page orientation: the counter-clockwise successor of the west arm is the south arm, the opposite slot is east, and the remaining slot is north. Calling the next slot north would be the reflection. The reflection is not a second generator. The earlier review says a reflection is not licensed; it does not choose these slot offsets. The offsets are this reading.

A frame is a choice of west slot `w` in `{1, 2, 3, 4}`. Forward applies when the over pair is `{west, east}`. It adds 1, modulo 3, once at each of the four incident ends. A loop through two of those ends receives 2. It exchanges the half-edges in the east and south slots and sets the over pair to `{north, south}`. The same frame run backward requires the over pair `{north, south}`, exchanges those slots again, and subtracts 1 at each end. Two frames match a given over pair in the forward direction, and the other two match it backward. They are the rotations of the printed picture. After one forward step the same frame is a backward match.

The four printed 1's are the arm contributions. A blank arm is not how C is drawn. SUB is what lets those four localized 1's be stored as one integer per edge. Inferred: an edge that meets the vertex twice receives both 1's. The outside ends of the four edges stay where they are. Read from the figure, via the curl: the edge that left locally east leaves the patch to the south, and the edge that left locally south leaves the patch to the east.

No order of repetition is claimed. The scan does not say that three or six forward steps are the identity.

The two forward frames are the two choices of which over end is west. On a positive vertex stored by the normal adapter, slot 4 is the incoming over end and lies on the east of the Fig. 3.4 arrow picture, and slot 2 is the outgoing over end and lies on the west. That placement is read from the convention page's account of Fig. 3.4, which is draft. The page-aligned seating therefore takes west at slot 2 and exchanges slots 3 and 4. The other seating is that picture rotated 180°. Both are returned. On every registered closed example, both seatings keep the known H₁, one boundary sphere, and reversing dual pairs, on the stored graph and on its canonical representative. An earlier run reported that the page-aligned stored graph failed the oriented boundary while the canonical representative passed. That split was a defect in `thickening_boundary`: the count depended on the numbering of the half-edge ids. The defect is fixed. The moves were not changed. The implementation check of 2026-11-22 did not see the split, because the split was not in the moves. The moves do not call `subsegment_sum`. Each touched end is added with `colour_mod3`, which is the same reduction.

## MP

Positive MP is the direction drawn in Fig. 2.4, from two vertices to three. Read from the text before [BP97, Def. 3.4.8]: the direction that increases the number of vertices is called a positive MP-move. The same definition says the positive move adds one disc. The central edge has distinct ends, colour 0, and both ends over. At the left vertex the central end occupies the east slot. At the right vertex it occupies the west slot. Both choices of which endpoint is the left vertex are returned. They are the printed picture and its 180° rotation. A 90° turn that keeps the central end in the east slot of the left vertex is the same match. Reflections are not returned.

Port correspondence, read from the right-hand picture, with the counter-clockwise compass above. Outside stubs on the left of the figure have no digits, so the printed contributions are on the right-hand picture:

| Left port | Right port | Printed contribution |
|---|---|---|
| A west | middle west | none |
| A north | top west | none |
| A south | bottom west | none |
| B east | middle east | none |
| B north | top north | +1 |
| B south | bottom south | −1 |

The central edge is deleted. The new edge from top south to middle north is −1. The new edge from middle south to bottom north is +1. The new edge from top east to bottom east is 0. Top and bottom are vertical over. The middle vertex is horizontal over. New vertices are stored counter-clockwise from their west slot, in the order top, middle, bottom, after the vertices that were not A or B.

Inferred from SUB, because the left stubs are unlabelled rather than labelled 0 as a background colour: a surviving edge is changed by the sum of the printed contributions at the ends that the move touches. An end with no digit contributes 0. Fig. 3.10 panel 1 has background −1 on the three outer edges and is the worked case. The upper edge receives 0 at A and +1 at B, so −1 + 1 = 0. The inner edge receives 0 at A and −1 at B, so −1 − 1 = +1. The outer edge receives 0 at both moved ends and stays −1. That is panel 2. The 180° witness exchanges those two results. The three outer edges of panel 1 have the same colour, so after vertex renumbering and slot rotation the two witnesses are one coloured o-graph. Their stored colours differ before that renumbering. The inverse subtracts those contributions and restores a colour-0 central edge with both ends over.

The inverse pattern is the right-hand picture read backwards: a non-loop colour-0 edge in the east slots of two vertical-over vertices, a middle horizontal-over vertex whose north edge to the top vertex is −1 and whose south edge to the bottom vertex is +1. Half-edges are compacted to `1:4n` after either direction because the coloured o-graph mate is a fixed-point-free involution on that range. Comparison of an MP round trip uses the canonical coloured o-graph. A C round trip keeps the half-edge ids.

On the registered closed examples every forward MP witness has boundary Euler characteristic 2, one orientable component, every dual pair reversing, and the same H₁ as the source. The stored graph and the canonical representative agree. The earlier stored Euler characteristics −4, −6, −2, and 0 were the same half-edge numbering defect, since fixed. One orientation of the two-vertex lens space is a different canonical graph from the catalogue image of that edge, and it too has boundary Euler characteristic 2. The ports were not changed.

## Normal MP

The comparison with `verify_ograph_move` is not the definition of MP. The coloured rule is the figure. For each registered closed example, each forward MP candidate that the verifier accepts is recoloured on both sides by `normal_coloured_ograph`. The coloured matches of the source are hashed with `coloured_ograph_hash` and compared with the recoloured target. A direct match is expected only when the moved edge is already the Fig. 2.4 central edge. Other catalogue patterns are the C–MP–C conjugates described in the proof of [BP97, Prop. 3.5.1]. The test searches one forward C before the coloured MP, and one forward C after a direct coloured MP, and only on examples with at most three vertices. Measured direct matches are the two-vertex lens space, both accepted catalogue moves; two of the six accepted moves on the four-vertex product; and two of the four accepted moves on the two-vertex product. The three-vertex lens space and the quaternion space have accepted catalogue moves and no direct coloured match, and one forward C on one side does not reach them either. A separate search, outside that test, applies one C of either direction, then the coloured MP, then one C of either direction. On the three-vertex lens space it reaches the single catalogue hash, by two sequences: a forward C at frame 2, a coloured MP, and an inverse C at frame 2. On the quaternion space it reaches both catalogue hashes, by six sequences of the same shape. On the two-vertex product the same search does not reach the two catalogue images that are already outside the direct matches. The one-vertex sphere and the Poincaré sphere have no coloured MP site; the Poincaré search does not try a conjugating C. Disagreement is a finding, not a reason to change the ports. After the half-edge numbering fix, a shared canonical graph has one boundary Euler characteristic. The earlier pair of values 2 and 0 on the two-vertex product was that defect.

## Sanity counts

On every graph the tests pass to `move_sanity`, the counted objects are: `n` vertices, `2n` edges, `4n` half-edges, `6n` germs, `d` discs, Euler characteristic `d − n`, boundary length `6n`, each boundary component a closed orientable surface of Euler characteristic at most 2, total boundary Euler characteristic `2(d − n)`, `2n` dual face pairs each orientation-reversing, and `d` dual edges. These counts use candidate B. For a graph obtained by `normal_coloured_ograph` from a registered closed example, the same function is compared with `d = n + 1`, Euler characteristic 1, one boundary sphere, and with `ograph_h1` of the normal presentation. H₁ and the boundary are compared on the stored graph and on the canonical representative. Both pass on every tested C image and every tested MP image. A mismatch with the known value is reported with the example; the move is not altered to remove it. Renaming vertices or rotating slots leaves the canonical graph, the homology, and the boundary Euler characteristic unchanged. The earlier failure of that last clause was the half-edge numbering defect in `thickening_boundary`, since fixed.

## Zero-vertex targets of the normal-move verifier

This is the request from the normal-move lane, not a Fig. 2.4 rule. [BP97, §1.4] N1 puts a zero-vertex graph outside the closed normal o-graphs. MATLAB `isClosed` describes that class. For an empty orientation its C2 clause is `length(gc)<=1`, which is not the Figure 1.2 graph, and its C3 clause is the disk-table height equal to `NV+1`. Julia `is_closed` returns `(false, (true, true, false))` on the empty link. `closedness_witness` on the empty state instead stores the MST contract `criteria == (true, true, true)` with `n1 == false`. `verify_ograph_move` used to accept a side when `all(criteria)` held, so the inverse of S on the closed four-vertex word `[1, 2, -3, -4, 4, 3, -2, -1]` with all signs −1 was accepted onto the empty word. The gate now reads `n1` and returns `:source_outside_n1` or `:target_outside_n1`. It does not accept that contract through `all(criteria)`, and it does not add an MST-circuit acceptance branch: `is_closed` already rejects the target. The verifier version string stays `ograph-move-source-replay/3` because the serialized closedness fields of nonempty witnesses are unchanged. The one-vertex link `[[-1, 1]]` with sign `[-1]` still has a one-vertex inverse, and that inverse remains inside the class.
